# Adolf Reinach

Adolf Reinach (1883 to 1917) gave the first systematic account of how claims,
obligations, and other legal entities come into being through social acts such as
promising, waiving, and enacting. His *Apriori Foundations of the Civil Law* (1913)
anticipates speech act theory by half a century and supplies the philosophical
core of several Foundry design patterns.

## Works by Reinach

**The Apriori Foundations of the Civil Law**

- "Die apriorischen Grundlagen des bürgerlichen Rechtes," *Jahrbuch für Philosophie und phänomenologische Forschung* 1 (1913): 685 to 847.
- English translation by John F. Crosby, *Aletheia* 3 (1983), with Crosby's biography of Reinach and his essay "Reinach's Discovery of the Social Acts." Stephan Kinsella describes the volume and links a scan at [stephankinsella.com](https://stephankinsella.com/2009/11/reinach-apriori-foundations-of-the-civil-law/) and on [Mises Wire](https://mises.org/wire/adolf-reinachs-apriori-foundations-civil-law).
- *The Apriori Foundations of the Civil Law, Along with the Lecture "Concerning Phenomenology,"* trans. John F. Crosby (Frankfurt: Ontos, 2012; now [De Gruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110329803/html)).

**Other writings**

- *Über den Ursachenbegriff im geltenden Strafrecht* (Leipzig: J. A. Barth, 1905), Reinach's dissertation. English: "On the Concept of Causality in the Criminal Law," trans. Berit Brogaard, [*Libertarian Papers* 1, no. 35 (2009)](https://libertarianpapers.org/35-concept-causality-criminal-law/).
- "Kant's Interpretation of Hume's Problem," trans. J. N. Mohanty, *Southwestern Journal of Philosophy* 7, no. 2 (1976): 161 to 188.
- *Sämtliche Werke: Textkritische Ausgabe in 2 Bänden*, ed. Karl Schuhmann and Barry Smith (Munich: Philosophia, 1989).

## On Reinach

**Overviews**

- "Adolf Reinach," [*Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reinach/), with a [linked bibliography](https://philpapers.org/sep/reinach/) on PhilPapers.
- Marietta Auer, Paul B. Miller, Henry E. Smith, and James Toomey, eds., [*Reinach and the Foundations of Private Law*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/reinach-and-the-foundations-of-private-law) (Cambridge University Press, 2025; open access).

**Social ontology and speech acts**

- Kevin Mulligan, ed., *Speech Act and Sachverhalt: Reinach and the Foundations of Realist Phenomenology* (Dordrecht: Nijhoff, 1987).
- Barry Smith, ["An Essay on Material Necessity,"](https://ontology.buffalo.edu/smith/articles/reinach.html) in Philip Hanson and Bruce Hunter, eds., *Return of the A Priori*, *Canadian Journal of Philosophy* Supplementary Volume 18 (1993): 301 to 322.
- Barry Smith and Wojciech Żełaniec, ["Laws of Essence or Constitutive Rules? Reinach vs. Searle on the Ontology of Social Entities,"](https://philarchive.org/rec/SMILOE) in Francesca De Vecchi, ed., *Eidetica del Diritto e Ontologia Sociale: Il Realismo di Adolf Reinach* (Milan: Mimesis, 2012): 83 to 108.
- Kimberly Baltzer-Jaray, "Phenomenological Jurisprudence: A Reinterpretation of Adolf Reinach's Jahrbuch Essay," in J. Aaron Simmons and J. Edward Hackett, eds., *Phenomenology for the Twenty-First Century* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2016).

**Causation, property, and Austrian law and economics**

The Winter 2004 issue of the *Quarterly Journal of Austrian Economics* (vol. 7, no. 4) gathered papers on Reinach and the a priori foundations of law:

- Stephan Kinsella and Patrick Tinsley, ["Causation and Aggression,"](https://mises.org/quarterly-journal-austrian-economics/causation-and-aggression) 97 to 112; revised in Kinsella, *Legal Foundations of a Free Society* (Houston: Papinian Press, 2023).
- Hans-Hermann Hoppe, "Property, Causality, and Liability," 87 to 95; reprinted in Hoppe, *The Great Fiction*, 2nd ed. (Auburn, Ala.: Mises Institute, 2021).
- Jörg Guido Hülsmann, "The A Priori Foundations of Property Economics," 41 to 68.
- Larry J. Sechrest, "Praxeology, Economics, and Law: Issues and Implications," 19 to 40.
- Walter Block, "Austrian Law and Economics: The Contributions of Adolf Reinach and Murray Rothbard," 69 to 85.
- Leo Zaibert, "Toward Meta-Politics," 113 to 128.
