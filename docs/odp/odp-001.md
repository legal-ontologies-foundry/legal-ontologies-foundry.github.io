## Intent

Legal information systems routinely use one word for three different things.
"The lease" may mean the signing that took place on 1 March, the terms the
parties agreed to, or the stapled pages in a drawer. These differ in kind: the
signing is over, the terms persist, and the pages can be burned without the
terms ceasing to bind. This pattern separates the **legal act**, the **legal
content** it establishes, and the **legal documents** that carry that content,
so that every other Foundry pattern can refer to them unambiguously.

## Competency questions

1. Which legal act established this legal content, and when?
2. Which persons or organizations participated in that act?
3. Under what pre-existing legal content (an enabling statute, a procedural
   rule) was the act performed?
4. Which documents carry this legal content?
5. Do these two documents carry the same legal content?

## Solution

### Classes

| Label | IRI | BFO parent | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| legal act | `ODP001_0001000` | process | A process, having one or more persons or organizations as participants, that a legal system recognizes as bringing into being, modifying, or terminating legally significant entities. |
| legal content | `ODP001_0001001` | generically dependent continuant | A generically dependent continuant that is established by some legal act and that specifies legally significant entities, such as the provisions of a statute, the terms of a contract, or the holding of a judgment. |
| legal document | `ODP001_0001002` | material entity | A material entity that is the carrier of some legal content. |
| enactment | `ODP001_0001003` | legal act | A legal act in which a body with legislative authority establishes legal content of general application. |
| contract formation | `ODP001_0001004` | legal act | A legal act in which two or more parties establish legal content that binds them to one another. |

### Relations

| Label | IRI | Domain | Range | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| establishes | `ODP001_0001100` | legal act | legal content | A relation between a legal act and the legal content that comes into being through that act. |
| established by | `ODP001_0001101` | legal content | legal act | Inverse of *establishes*. |
| performed under | `ODP001_0001102` | legal act | legal content | A relation between a legal act and pre-existing legal content that authorizes or governs it. |

The pattern reuses BFO *has participant* (BFO:0000057) for the parties to an
act and BFO *is carrier of* (BFO:0000101) between a document and its content.

### Axioms (Manchester syntax)

```
legal act       SubClassOf  process and (has participant some material entity)
legal content   SubClassOf  generically dependent continuant
legal document  SubClassOf  material entity and (is carrier of some legal content)
enactment          SubClassOf  legal act and (establishes some legal content)
contract formation SubClassOf  legal act and (establishes some legal content)
enactment DisjointWith contract formation
```

### Grounding rationale (LOF-P-004)

- **Legal act as process.** A signing, a vote, or the delivery of a judgment
  unfolds in time and has temporal parts. It is over once it is complete,
  while what it brings about persists.
- **Legal content as generically dependent continuant.** The same statute
  exists in the official gazette, in every printed code, and on every server
  that stores it, and it survives the destruction of any one copy. That is
  the defining feature of a generically dependent continuant.
- **Legal document as material entity.** The particular signed paper, or the
  particular stored file, is a material thing that carries the content.
  Digital documents are treated as patterns in material storage media.

## Example

The signing of a residential lease (hypothetical parties):

```turtle
ex:leaseSigning a odp1:0001004 ;          # contract formation
    obo:BFO_0000057 ex:ana , ex:northside ;   # has participant
    odp1:0001100 ex:leaseTerms ;              # establishes
    odp1:0001102 ex:tenantCode .              # performed under

ex:leaseTerms a odp1:0001001 .               # legal content

ex:copyHeldByAna       a odp1:0001002 ; obo:BFO_0000101 ex:leaseTerms .
ex:copyHeldByNorthside a odp1:0001002 ; obo:BFO_0000101 ex:leaseTerms .
```

The two copies are distinct material entities carrying one and the same
legal content. The full example is in
`lof-odp-003/src/examples/lease-example.ttl`.

## Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)

| This pattern | Akoma Ntoso / ELI (FRBR-derived) | IAO |
|---|---|---|
| legal content | Work and Expression levels | information content entity (IAO:0000030), close match |
| legal document | Item level | none. IAO *document* (IAO:0000310) is an information content entity, and so corresponds to legal content |
| legal act | lifecycle events | none |

## Consequences and limitations

- The pattern does not distinguish versions of legal content (an amended
  statute versus the original). The FRBR Work and Expression distinction
  collapses here into a single class, which is a deliberate simplification
  for a first pattern.
- *Legal act* requires at least one participant. Acts by operation of law
  with no human participant, if there are any, fall outside it.
- *Enactment* and *contract formation* are illustrative subclasses. Domain
  ontologies will add many more (adjudication, conveyance, delegation).

## Open questions for the founding coordinators

1. Should the Foundry import IAO and place legal content under IAO
   *information content entity*, rather than directly under generically
   dependent continuant with a mapping? Importing IAO increases
   interoperability with OBO, at the cost of an extra dependency.
2. Should the Work and Expression distinction be modeled in this pattern,
   or deferred to a later versioning pattern?
3. Is the treatment of digital documents as patterns in material media
   acceptable, or does it need a pattern of its own?
