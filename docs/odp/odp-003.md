## Intent

Wesley Hohfeld showed that the word "right" covers four different legal
positions, each paired with a correlative held by another party:

| Position | Correlative | Order |
|---|---|---|
| claim | duty | first-order: what parties must or may do |
| liberty | no-claim | first-order |
| power | liability | second-order: who can change first-order positions |
| immunity | disability | second-order |

This pattern models the eight positions, their correlative pairing, their
holders, the legal content that grounds them, and the legal acts in which
powers are exercised. It gives every Foundry ontology a common vocabulary for
rights and duties that is more precise than the word "right" alone.

## Competency questions

1. What duties does this person hold, and to whom are they owed?
2. What is the correlative of this claim, and who holds it?
3. In virtue of what legal content does this position exist?
4. Which legal acts were exercises of this power?
5. Does this position survive the assignment of the claim, or the death of
   its holder?

### Axioms (Manchester syntax, common to both options)

```
legal position SubClassOf (held by some material entity)
                      and (grounded in some legal content)
                      and (correlative of some legal position)
claim SubClassOf  first-order legal position
            and (correlative of some duty) and (correlative of only duty)
duty  SubClassOf  first-order legal position
            and (correlative of some claim) and (correlative of only claim)
... likewise for the other three pairs ...
DisjointClasses: claim, duty, liberty, no-claim, power, liability, immunity, disability
correlative of: Symmetric
```

## The placement decision

The two options differ in two axioms only.

| | **Option A** (editors' file) | **Option B** (`alternatives/`) |
|---|---|---|
| *legal position* SubClassOf | specifically dependent continuant (BFO:0000020) | generically dependent continuant (BFO:0000031) |
| *held by* | functional subproperty of BFO *inheres in* | ordinary relation, not functional |
| A position is... | a feature of its holder, like a role | a piece of legal content's upshot, related to a holder |
| Can a position change holders? | No. It ends when its holder ceases to hold it. | Yes. The same position can pass to a new holder. |
| Assignment of a claim | the old claim ends; a new claim begins in the assignee | the same claim is now held by the assignee |
| Death of a debtor | the duty ends; the estate bears a new duty | the same duty passes to the estate |
| Fit with ODP-002 | uniform: roles and positions both inhere in bearers | positions and roles are of different kinds |
| Fit with ODP-001 | positions are distinct from the content grounding them | positions are close in kind to legal content |

*Held by* is functional in Option A because BFO's axioms state that a
specifically dependent continuant has exactly one bearer. BFO's OWL file does
not encode this, so the pattern states it explicitly.

### A test that separates them

The repository includes a worked example (a residential lease) and an
assignment test, in which the landlord assigns its claim to rent to a
collection agency, and the agency is said to hold *the same* claim.
`make test-options` reasons over both:

| | Lease example | Same claim after assignment |
|---|---|---|
| Option A | consistent | **inconsistent** |
| Option B | consistent | consistent |

So the choice is not merely metaphysical. It determines how every Foundry
ontology must represent assignment, succession, subrogation, and novation.
Under Option A, these are all modeled as the ending of one position and the
beginning of another, linked by a legal act. Under Option B, they are changes
in the holder of a single persisting position.

### Considerations

**For Option A.** It treats legal positions uniformly with legal roles
(ODP-002). It keeps a clear line between the content of the law, which is
generically dependent, and what the law brings about in particular persons,
which is specifically dependent. It also matches ordinary legal talk of a
person *having* a duty in the way they have a role. Novation, where a new
obligation replaces the old, fits it naturally.

**For Option B.** Legal practice often treats a claim as a transferable
asset, the same claim passing from assignor to assignee. Option B represents
that directly, without multiplying positions. It also aligns with approaches
that treat obligations as directive information content, such as the Common
Core Ontologies' directive information content entities.

**Declaration of interest.** The drafter's own work (*A Structural Ontology of
the Law*) treats obligations as specifically dependent continuants, as in
Option A. Option A is placed in the editors' file only so that the pattern has
a working default; it carries no presumption.

## Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)

The eight positions follow Hohfeld's *Fundamental Legal Conceptions* (1913,
1917). LKIF-Core and LegalRuleML represent obligations and permissions as
deontic modalities or rule components, rather than as entities with holders,
so their mapping to this pattern is partial. It will be documented once the
placement question is settled.

## Consequences and limitations

- The pattern does not say *what* a position is about: the action type a duty
  requires (paying rent) or a liberty permits. Referring to types of action is
  a known difficulty in OWL; the options are listed below.
- OWL cannot express that correlatives are held by *different* parties, or
  that a claim and its correlative duty concern the same action. These are
  documented constraints, not enforced ones.
- Hohfeld's jural opposites (claim versus no-claim in the same holder) are not
  modeled, because they relate a position to the absence of another.

## Open questions for the founding coordinators

1. **Placement.** Option A or Option B? (The central question.)
2. **Action types.** How should a position indicate the conduct it concerns?
   Options: (a) a relation to a plan specification or directive information
   content entity, following IAO and CCO; (b) a relation to a process
   universal via OWL punning; (c) a relation to a process profile. Each has
   costs for reasoning.
3. **Realizability.** Should duties, liberties, and powers be treated as
   realizable entities, realized in compliance, in acting, and in the
   exercise of the power? This is available only under Option A.
4. Should correlatives be modeled as parts of a single relational whole (a
   legal relation between two parties), rather than as two linked positions?
