## Intent

ODP-003 says that legal positions are held by persons or organizations, and
ODP-002 that legal roles inhere in them. Neither says what it takes to be the
kind of thing that can hold a position at all. Legal systems answer that
question explicitly, and their answers diverge: corporations are persons
almost everywhere; Hindu idols are persons in Indian law; the Whanganui River
has been a person in New Zealand law since 2017; in the past, enslaved human
beings were denied personality. Every system also separates being a person
from being able to act: an infant holds property but cannot sell it, and a
corporation holds rights but acts only through natural persons.

This pattern separates three things that the word "capacity" runs together:

- **Legal personality** (passive capacity): eligibility to hold legal
  positions at all.
- **Capacity to act** (active capacity): eligibility to change one's own
  positions by one's own legal acts.
- **Legal roles** (ODP-002): the particular standings a person occupies, such
  as tenant, trustee, or director.

It adds **representation**, through which persons without capacity to act,
and organizations, perform legal acts, and it relativizes personality and
capacity to a legal order (ODP-007), since one order's person need not be
another's.

## Competency questions

1. Which entities are legal persons, of what kind (natural or juristic), and
   in which legal orders?
2. By what act, if any, was this entity's juristic personality conferred, and
   by what act was it ended?
3. Does this person have capacity to act, and in which orders?
4. Through whom does this person act, in what representative role?
5. Which holders of legal positions in a knowledge base are not recorded as
   having legal personality?

Questions 1 to 4 are answered by the reasoner and SPARQL. Question 5 is
answered by a SHACL shape (`src/shapes/holder-personality.ttl`, severity
warning).

## Solution

### Classes

| Label | IRI | BFO parent | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| legal personality | `ODP009_0001000` | legal role | A legal role in virtue of which its bearer is eligible to hold legal positions. |
| natural legal personality | `ODP009_0001001` | legal personality | Legal personality that a legal order attributes to a human being as such. |
| juristic personality | `ODP009_0001002` | legal personality | Legal personality that a legal order attributes to an entity other than an individual human being. |
| legal person | `ODP009_0001003` | material entity (defined) | A material entity that is the bearer of some legal personality. |
| capacity to act | `ODP009_0001004` | legal role | A legal role in virtue of which its bearer's own legal acts can bring about legal effects for that bearer. |
| representative role | `ODP009_0001005` | legal role | A legal role in virtue of which its bearer's legal acts, performed within it, bring about legal effects for some other legal person. |
| incorporation | `ODP009_0001006` | role conferral act (defined) | A role conferral act that confers juristic personality. |

### Relations

| Label | IRI | Domain | Range | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| on behalf of | `ODP009_0001100` | representative role | legal person | A relation between a representative role and the legal person for whom its bearer acts. |

### Axioms (Manchester syntax)

```
legal personality  SubClassOf  legal role                         # LOF-ODP-002
natural legal personality, juristic personality  SubClassOf  legal personality
natural legal personality  DisjointWith  juristic personality
legal person       EquivalentTo  material entity and (bearer of some legal personality)
capacity to act    SubClassOf  legal role and (inheres in only legal person)
representative role SubClassOf legal role and (on behalf of some legal person)
incorporation      EquivalentTo  role conferral act and (confers some juristic personality)

```

Version 0.1.0 also had *legal position SubClassOf held by only legal person*.
The [regime test suite](regime-tests.md) showed it to be false in law: in
*Mabo v Queensland (No 2)* the Meriam people hold native title communally,
without legal personality, and unincorporated associations hold property
through their members. The axiom classified such holders as legal persons. It
is replaced by a SHACL warning that reports holders with no recorded
personality, so that a knowledge base can confirm that the holding is communal
or record the missing personality.

*Recognized in* also applies to capacity to act (the OWL file declares no domain). Personality is recorded, not inferred from holding: a knowledge base states
that Ana bears natural legal personality, as it states that Northside bears
juristic personality.

### Grounding rationale (LOF-P-004)

- **Personality and capacity are roles.** A person acquires or loses legal
  personality, or capacity to act, with no change in physical make-up: at
  birth, on incorporation, at the age of majority, on a declaration of
  incapacity, on dissolution. That is the mark of a BFO role, as for legal
  roles in ODP-002, and both are placed under *legal role* so that ODP-002's
  conferral and termination acts apply to them unchanged. Capacity to act
  usually tracks age and mental competence, which are grounded in the
  bearer's make-up, but the law fixes where the line falls and moves it
  without any change in the bearer. It is therefore a role and not a
  disposition.
- **Legal person is a defined class of bearers.** *Legal person* is not a
  kind of role but a classification of material entities by the roles they
  bear, like *employee* or *patient* in other ontologies. This leaves the
  nature of the bearer open: a human being, an object aggregate of members,
  a river, a ship.
- **Organizations.** BFO has no settled account of organizations (ODP-002
  open question 3). The pattern needs only that a juristic person be a
  material entity bearing juristic personality, so any domain account that
  makes organizations material entities, for example object aggregates of
  their members, can be used.
- **Representation as a role.** An agent, guardian, or director has a
  standing that makes their acts count for another. That is a legal role of
  the representative, related to the represented person, not a feature of the
  represented person.

## Example

Northside is a company formed under the state's law and acting through its
manager, Ben; Ana has full capacity; Cal, Ana's co-tenant, is seventeen.
Excerpt:

```turtle
ex:northsidePersonality a odp9:0001002 ;      # juristic personality
    obo:BFO_0000197 ex:northside ;            # inheres in
    odp9:0001101 ex:stateOrder .              # recognized in
ex:northsideFormation a odp1:0001000 ;        # legal act
    odp2:0001100 ex:northsidePersonality .    # confers

ex:benManagerRole a odp9:0001005 ;            # representative role
    obo:BFO_0000197 ex:ben ;
    odp9:0001100 ex:northside .               # on behalf of
ex:leaseSigning obo:BFO_0000055 ex:benManagerRole .   # realizes

ex:anaCapacity a odp9:0001004 ;               # capacity to act
    obo:BFO_0000197 ex:ana .

ex:calPersonality a odp9:0001001 ;            # natural personality, no capacity recorded
    obo:BFO_0000197 ex:cal .
```

Inferred by HermiT, under both ODP-003 options: the filing of Northside's
certificate of formation is an *incorporation* (and so a *role conferral act*,
ODP-002); Northside and Ana are *legal persons*. The state legislature, which holds the
legislative power in ODP-004's example, is not inferred to be a legal person
merely by holding it; the SHACL shape reports it, which prompts the question
whether the power belongs to the state and is exercised by the legislature as
its organ (open question 2). Cal's signing of a co-tenancy rider is
voidable and is annulled by Cal's disaffirmance (ODP-001 0.3.0 proposal). The full
example is `src/examples/foundations-example.ttl`.

## Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)

| This pattern | FIBO (Business Entities) | LKIF-Core | Akoma Ntoso |
|---|---|---|---|
| legal person | legal person, close match | person classes, related | `TLCPerson`, `TLCOrganization`, related (references, not personality) |
| juristic personality | legal entity, related (FIBO classifies the entity, not the personality) | none | none |
| capacity to act | legally competent natural person, related (as a class of persons) | none | none |
| representative role | none | agent and role classes, related | `TLCRole`, related |
| incorporation | formation of a legal entity, related | none | none |

FIBO and LKIF classify persons; this pattern classifies the roles in virtue
of which they are persons, so mappings are between a class of bearers and a
class of roles and are *related* at best. Mappings to be verified against
current releases before publication as SSSOM.

## Consequences and limitations

- **No negative facts.** A person who lacks capacity to act is recorded by
  the absence of a capacity, which OWL cannot infer from (open world).
  Incapacity can be reported by SHACL, or recorded positively by a
  guardianship role held by another person *on behalf of* the incapacitated
  one.
- **Partial capacity.** Testamentary capacity, capacity to marry, capacity to
  contract for necessaries, and the like are subclasses of *capacity to act*
  in domain ontologies, with `dcterms:coverage` (LOF-P-010).
- **Beginning and end of personality.** Personality arising at birth by
  operation of law has no conferring act, which is ODP-002's existing gap for
  roles arising by operation of law. Posthumous interests (a deceased
  author's moral rights, the estate) are held by the estate or a
  representative, not by the deceased.
- **Artificial agents.** Whether an AI system can be a legal person is a live
  question in several orders. The pattern answers it structurally: if an
  order attributes personality to some material entity (the hardware, a
  registered system), the entity is a legal person in that order. Software as
  such is a generically dependent continuant and cannot bear a role in BFO,
  which is a substantive consequence (open question 4).
- **Animals and nature.** Protecting an animal or a river by a duty does not
  make it a person; it is what the duty is *directed at* (ODP-004). Only where
  an order attributes personality, as New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua
  (Whanganui River Claims Settlement) Act 2017 does, is the entity a person.
- **Jurisdiction.** Checked against the forms below (LOF-P-010):

| Form | How it is expressed |
|---|---|
| Corporation, foundation, association | juristic personality conferred by *incorporation*; organs in *representative roles* |
| State, international organization | juristic personality *recognized in* the international order |
| Minor, adult under guardianship | natural personality, no (or partial) capacity to act; a guardian's *representative role* |
| Trust (common law) | no personality; the trustee holds the positions in its own name, in a trustee role (ODP-002) specified by the trust instrument |
| Hindu deity (Indian law) | juristic personality borne by the idol, acting through its shebait in a representative role |
| River or natural feature as person | juristic personality borne by the feature, acting through appointed representatives |
| Foreign corporation | personality conferred in one order, *recognized in* another (ODP-007) |

## Open questions for the founding coordinators

1. **Only persons hold positions.** *(Resolved in this draft by the regime test
   suite: moved from an axiom to a SHACL warning, because communal holders
   hold positions without personality.)* Should the Foundry add a class for
   communal holding (a people, a clan, an association) so that the warning
   can be silenced where the holding is communal by law?
2. **Organs.** Should powers of organs (a legislature, a court) be recorded
   as held by the juristic person (the state) and exercised through a
   representative role, or as held by the organ?
3. **Personality under legal role.** Placing personality under ODP-002 *legal
   role* reuses conferral and termination, but ODP-002 defines *legal role* as
   inhering in "a person or organization", which becomes circular. Should
   ODP-002's definition be revised to "a material entity", with this pattern
   supplying what makes the bearer a person?
4. **Artificial agents.** Is the BFO consequence acceptable, that only a
   material entity (not a model or program) can be a legal person? It bears
   directly on proposals for electronic personhood.
5. **Capacity and powers.** Should the pattern state that a power can be
   exercised by its holder only if the holder has capacity to act, and
   otherwise only through a representative? That is a rule-layer constraint
   in OWL, but could be a SHACL shape.
