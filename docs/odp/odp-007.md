## Intent

ODP-001 to 006 say what legal content is, how positions arise from it, when
it is in force, and which item prevails over which. None of them says *whose*
law it is. Yet "is this valid?" is always a question asked within some legal
order: a Texas statute is valid in the Texas order and means nothing in the
French one; a customary rule is law in the international order whether or not
any state enacted it; a municipal ordinance is valid only if the state order
that created the municipality authorized it. Without legal orders, the
Foundry cannot say what validity is, cannot tell the law of one system from
another when data from both is combined, and cannot state the relation
between orders that legal pluralism is about.

This pattern adds the **legal order** as an entity, individuates it by its
**recognition criterion**, treats **validity as membership** in an order, and
infers membership in the two ways legal theory has identified: by satisfying
the order's criterion directly (static validity), and by arising from an act
performed under content that already belongs to the order (dynamic validity,
Kelsen's chain of validation). It relates orders by **embedding** (an order
existing by the authority of another) and **recognition** (an order giving
effect to another's content on its own authority), and gives orders and their
content **territorial and personal scope**.

Three distinctions carry most of the weight.

- **Belonging is not having effect.** Mexican law governs a Texas contract
  with a Mexican choice-of-law clause, so it has effect in the Texas order.
  It does not thereby become Texas law. The pattern keeps *belongs to* (whose
  law is this) apart from *has effect in* (what law governs here).
- **Embedding is not recognition.** A state order in a federation exists by
  the federation's authority; a foreign order does not exist by the authority
  of the order that recognizes it. In both cases the primary order's authority
  is presumed: the embedded or recognized content has effect on the primary
  order's terms.
- **"Jurisdiction" means three things.** The order whose law it is (here,
  *legal order*), the reach of that law over territory and persons (here,
  *territorial* and *personal scope*), and a body's authority to decide cases
  (an adjudicative power, [LOF-ODP-008](odp-008.md)). The pattern mints no
  class called *jurisdiction*.

## Competency questions

1. To which legal orders does this item of legal content belong, and on what
   basis: its criterion, or a chain of authorization back to content that
   satisfies it?
2. What is the recognition criterion of this order, and in what official
   practice is it maintained?
3. In which orders does this content have effect, and by what route:
   membership, embedding, or recognition?
4. Which orders are embedded in this order, and which orders does it
   recognize?
5. Over what territory, and over which persons, does this order or this item
   of content govern?
6. Which items of content in a knowledge base belong to no recorded order?

Questions 1 to 5 are answered by the reasoner and SPARQL; question 6 by a
SHACL shape on the reasoned graph (open world: OWL cannot infer that content
belongs to no order).

## Solution

### Classes

| Label | IRI | BFO parent | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| legal order | `ODP007_0001000` | generically dependent continuant | A generically dependent continuant consisting of the legal content that satisfies a given recognition criterion, together with that criterion, which persists while the content belonging to it changes. |
| recognition criterion | `ODP007_0001001` | generically dependent continuant | A generically dependent continuant that states the conditions under which legal content belongs to some legal order. |
| embedded legal order | `ODP007_0001002` | legal order (defined) | A legal order that is embedded in some other legal order. |
| legal territory | `ODP007_0001003` | site (defined) | A site that is the territorial scope of some legal order. |

### Relations

| Label | IRI | Domain | Range | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| belongs to | `ODP007_0001100` | legal content | legal order | A relation between legal content and a legal order of which it is a valid part. |
| has member content | `ODP007_0001101` | legal order | legal content | Inverse of *belongs to*. |
| has recognition criterion | `ODP007_0001102` | legal order | recognition criterion | A relation between a legal order and a recognition criterion that states conditions under which legal content belongs to it. |
| satisfies | `ODP007_0001103` | legal content | recognition criterion | A relation between legal content and a recognition criterion whose conditions it meets. |
| embedded in | `ODP007_0001104` | legal order | legal order | A relation between two legal orders in which the first exists by the authority of the second, and its content has effect in the second without belonging to it. Transitive. |
| recognizes | `ODP007_0001105` | legal order | legal order | A relation between two legal orders in which the first gives effect, on its own authority, to content belonging to the second. |
| has effect in | `ODP007_0001106` | legal content | legal order | A relation between legal content and a legal order in which it governs, whether because it belongs to the order, belongs to an order embedded in it, or belongs to an order it recognizes (inferred). |

| maintained by | `ODP007_0001109` | recognition criterion | process | A relation between a recognition criterion and a process of official practice in which it is accepted and applied. |
| void in | `ODP007_0001110` | purported legal act | legal order | A relation between a purported legal act and a legal order in which it brings about none of the effects it purports to bring about. Added after the [regime test suite](regime-tests.md). |

### Axioms (Manchester syntax)

```
legal order            SubClassOf  generically dependent continuant
                                   and (has recognition criterion some recognition criterion)
recognition criterion  SubClassOf  generically dependent continuant
embedded legal order   EquivalentTo legal order and (embedded in some legal order)
legal territory        EquivalentTo site and (inverse (has territorial scope) some legal order)

legal content  SubClassOf  belongs to some legal order          # open question 1

satisfies o inverse (has recognition criterion)     SubPropertyOf  belongs to   # static validity
arises from o performed under o belongs to         SubPropertyOf  belongs to   # dynamic validity
belongs to                                          SubPropertyOf  has effect in
belongs to o embedded in                            SubPropertyOf  has effect in
belongs to o inverse (recognizes)                   SubPropertyOf  has effect in
embedded in: Transitive

inverse (declares void) o performed under o belongs to   SubPropertyOf  void in
```

**Relative nullity.** The last chain was added after the
[regime test suite](regime-tests.md). One process can be void in one order and
a valid act in another: a wedding declared null by a church tribunal stands as
a civil marriage; Innocent III declared Magna Carta null in August 1215, and
England treated it as a grant. Recording such an act as an ODP-001 *void act*
is inconsistent with its validity elsewhere (the suite includes this as a
negative test). *Void in* relativizes nullity to an order, and is inferred
when the declaration of nullity is performed under content of that order: the
declaration speaks for its order and for no other.

*Has territorial scope* and *has personal scope* also apply to legal content (the OWL file declares no domain), since content can reach less or further than its order. *Arises from* and *performed under* are LOF-ODP-001 relations. The dynamic
validity chain is right-recursive, which OWL 2 DL permits: content arising
from an act performed under content that belongs to an order belongs to it,
however long the chain of authorization. A lease belongs to the state order
because it arises from a signing performed under the tenancy code, which
arises from an enactment performed under the constitution, which satisfies
the state's recognition criterion.

### Grounding rationale (LOF-P-004)

- **A legal order is a generically dependent continuant.** It is not the set
  of its norms, since a set cannot gain or lose members, and an order survives
  the repeal of every statute it had at its founding. Nor is it an aggregate
  of officials or institutions, which can be replaced wholesale while the law
  continues. It is a pattern, concretized in the practices of officials and
  the dispositions of those who follow it, and borne by no particular one of
  them. That is the mark of a generically dependent continuant, and it puts
  orders in the same category as the content that belongs to them.
- **Identity through its criterion.** An order persists while its criterion
  persists, including changes to the criterion made by the order's own rules
  of change. A change of criterion made outside them (a revolution, a
  secession) ends one order and begins another, even where most of the old
  content is carried over. The carried-over content then belongs to the new
  order by its new criterion.
- **The criterion is not, in general, legal content.** In Hart's account the
  rule of recognition is not valid by any further criterion; it exists in the
  accepting practice of officials, which *maintained by* records. Where an
  order states its criterion in its own content (a supremacy clause, Article
  38(1) of the ICJ Statute), that content can also be asserted to be legal
  content, as LOF-ODP-006 does for enacted priority principles. The pattern
  does not decide between Hart and Kelsen: Kelsen's basic norm can be recorded
  as a recognition criterion that is maintained by no practice.
- **Validity as membership, not as a quality of content.** Validity is not a
  feature an item of content has in itself. The same text is valid in one
  order and not in another, so validity is a relation between content and an
  order. The pattern adds no "valid" class.
- **Territory as site.** A territory (land, territorial waters, airspace) is
  where material entities are located, and moves with the earth, so it is a
  BFO site rather than a spatial region.

## Example

Continuing the residential lease of ODP-001 to 004 (`lease-chain.ttl`). The
state order is embedded in a federal order, which recognizes the
international order. Excerpt:

```turtle
ex:stateOrder a odp7:0001000 ;                 # legal order
    odp7:0001102 ex:stateCriterion ;           # has recognition criterion
    odp7:0001107 ex:stateTerritory ;           # has territorial scope
    odp7:0001104 ex:federalOrder .             # embedded in
ex:stateCriterion a odp7:0001001 ;
    odp7:0001109 ex:stateOfficialPractice .    # maintained by

ex:constitution odp7:0001103 ex:stateCriterion .      # satisfies
ex:tenantCodeEnactment odp1:0001102 ex:constitution . # performed under

ex:federalOrder a odp7:0001000 ;
    odp7:0001105 intl:internationalOrder .     # recognizes

intl:internationalOrder a odp7:0001000 ;
    odp7:0001102 intl:art38Criterion .         # ICJ Statute Art. 38(1)(b)
intl:immunityPractice a odp1:0001008 ;         # norm-generating practice
    odp1:0001105 intl:immunityRule .           # gives rise to
intl:immunityRule odp7:0001103 intl:art38Criterion .
```

Inferred by HermiT, under both ODP-003 options:

| Content | Inferred | Route |
|---|---|---|
| state constitution | belongs to the state order | satisfies its criterion |
| tenancy code | belongs to the state order | enacted under the constitution |
| lease terms | belongs to the state order; has effect in the federal order | signed under the tenancy code; the state order is embedded in the federal one |
| customary rule of diplomatic immunity | belongs to the international order; has effect in the federal order | arises from state practice satisfying Art. 38(1)(b); the federal order recognizes the international one |
| judgment for possession, binding holding (ODP-008) | belong to the state order | arise from adjudicative acts performed under state content |

The customary rule is *not* inferred to have effect in the state order:
embedding and recognition run one way, and nothing in the example says the
state order recognizes the international one. The state is a *legal
territory* and the state order an *embedded legal order*, both by
classification. The full example is `src/examples/foundations-example.ttl`.

## Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)

| This pattern | Akoma Ntoso | ELI | LegalRuleML | LKIF-Core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legal order | `FRBRcountry` names the issuing jurisdiction of a work, related | jurisdiction metadata on legal resources, related | `Jurisdiction` metadata on rules, related | none |
| belongs to | none | none | association of a rule with its jurisdiction, related | none |
| recognition criterion | none | none | none | none |
| has territorial scope | `FRBRcountry`, related | jurisdiction metadata, related | `Jurisdiction`, related | none |

All three standards record the jurisdiction of a document or rule as
metadata, a label naming a place or system. None treats a legal order as an
entity with a criterion of membership, and none distinguishes membership from
effect. The mappings are therefore at most *related*, and are to be verified
against current releases before they are published as SSSOM.

## Consequences and limitations

- **Membership is the default, not a proof.** The dynamic validity chain
  infers membership from authorization as recorded. It does not check that
  the act stayed within its authority. Ultra vires content is handled by
  nullity (ODP-001 0.3.0 proposal): a void act gives rise to nothing, so nothing
  inherits membership from it. If the act is recorded as a legal act and is
  in fact ultra vires, the knowledge base is wrong, and adjudication
  ([ODP-008](odp-008.md)) is how such errors are corrected.
- **No derogation of membership.** Content that belongs to an order but has
  been held invalid by a court with authority to do so is recorded with
  [ODP-005](odp-005.md) (its period in force ends) or as a void act, not by
  removing membership, which OWL cannot do.
- **Effect without scope.** *Has effect in* says that content governs in an
  order, not for which cases. Which Mexican provisions a Texas court applies
  under a choice-of-law clause is a question for the rule layer.
- **Subject-matter scope is not modeled.** The reach of content over types of
  conduct (tax, family, criminal) is type-level and belongs to the rule layer,
  like the scope of a derogation in ODP-006.
- **Disputed territory and competing orders.** Two orders can claim the same
  territory or the same persons. The pattern records both claims and does not
  treat the overlap as an inconsistency; reporting it is for SHACL.
- **Jurisdiction.** Nothing in the pattern assumes a state. It has been
  checked against the forms below (LOF-P-010):

| Form | How it is expressed |
|---|---|
| Unitary state | one order with a criterion; statutes belong by chain |
| Federation | state orders *embedded in* the federal order |
| Supranational order (EU) | the EU order; member state orders *recognize* it; precedence by ODP-006 *primacy of an overriding legal order* |
| International law | an order whose criterion is ICJ Statute Art. 38(1); custom by *satisfies*, treaties by chain from the *pacta sunt servanda* content |
| Personal laws (religious courts within a state) | a religious order with *has personal scope* over the community, *recognized* (or *embedded*) by the state order |
| Indigenous and customary orders | an order whose content arises from norm-generating practice; recognition by the state order where the state gives it effect |
| Conflict of laws | the forum's order *recognizes* the foreign order |
| Revolution, secession | a new order with a new criterion; carried-over content *satisfies* the new criterion |

- **Succession of orders.** The suite modeled England from 1215 to the
  present as one order. A pattern for one order succeeding another (state
  succession, revolution, union) with carried-over content is left open
  (open question 6).

## Open questions for the founding coordinators

1. **Is all legal content in some order?** The axiom *legal content SubClassOf
   belongs to some legal order* makes it so. It excludes legal content outside
   every order (natural law as such, lex mercatoria on some accounts). Keep
   the axiom, weaken it to a SHACL warning, or drop it?
2. **Is a legal order a generically dependent continuant?** The alternatives
   are an object aggregate (of officials or institutions) or a new category.
   The choice affects ODP-002 open question 1 (offices), which has the same
   shape.
3. **Authority relative to an order.** ODP-006 open question 3 asks whether
   authority should be relative to a legal order. This pattern makes that
   possible: an authoritative determination could be required to belong to an
   order, and *prevails over* could be relativized to it. Should ODP-006 be
   revised accordingly?
4. **Embedding versus recognition.** The pattern treats embedding as giving
   effect without membership. Some federal systems incorporate state law into
   federal law for some purposes. Is a third relation (incorporation) needed,
   or is *belongs to* asserted directly in those cases enough?
5. **Recognition criterion as legal content.** Should a criterion stated in a
   constitution be asserted to be legal content, which makes the constitution
   self-validating, or should the stated criterion and the practised
   criterion be kept apart?

6. **Succession of orders.** Should the Foundry relate an order to the order
   it succeeds (England, Great Britain, the United Kingdom; a state after
   revolution or secession), and say what content is carried over?
