## Intent

ODP-001 to 004 say what legal content is and how a position comes to exist,
but not when. A legal question is almost always a question at a time: was the
Directive or the Regulation the law on 15 March 2017; did the controller
notify within seventy-two hours; which text was in force when the contract was
signed. This pattern gives legal content its periods, records the acts that
change it, distinguishes the consolidated texts people actually read from the
law they consolidate, and gives positions the periods within which they must
be discharged.

Three distinctions carry most of the weight.

- **In force is not applies.** The General Data Protection Regulation entered
  into force on 24 May 2016 and applied from 25 May 2018. Retroactive content
  applies to a period before it is in force. Repealed content goes on
  governing facts that occurred while it applied.
- **Content is succeeded, not changed.** An amending act establishes content
  of its own, which *succeeds* the amended content in whole or in part. The
  amended content is unchanged; what changes is which content is in force.
- **A consolidation is not, by default, law.** EU consolidated texts have no
  legal force. A United States Code title enacted as positive law does.

## Competency questions

1. What legal content was in force at a given time?
2. What legal content applies to a given occurrence, and what was in force but
   did not apply to it?
3. What amended or repealed this content, in what act, and when did its force
   end?
4. Which consolidated version states the law during a given period, and what
   content does it combine?
5. Which positions were not discharged within the period they were due
   within, computed from what occurrence, and what positions does the end of
   that period trigger?

All five are answered by SPARQL (`src/sparql/`).

## Solution

### Classes

| Label | IRI | BFO parent | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| amending act | `ODP005_0001000` | legal act (defined) | A legal act that establishes legal content succeeding some legal content established earlier, or that ends, suspends, or alters the force or scope of such content. |
| repeal | `ODP005_0001001` | legal act (defined) | A legal act that establishes that some legal content established earlier ceases to be in force. |
| consolidated version | `ODP005_0001002` | generically dependent continuant | A generically dependent continuant that combines some legal content with the content succeeding it in part, as they stand during some temporal region. |
| compliance period | `ODP005_0001003` | temporal region (defined) | A temporal region within which some first-order legal position is to be discharged. |

### Relations

| Label | IRI | Domain | Range | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| amends | `ODP005_0001100` | legal act | legal content | The act establishes content succeeding the content, or ends, suspends, or alters its force or scope. |
| repeals | `ODP005_0001101` | legal act | legal content | Subproperty of *amends*: the act establishes that the content ceases to be in force. |
| succeeds | `ODP005_0001108` | legal content | legal content | The first replaces the second, in whole or in part, from the time the first is in force. |
| in force during | `ODP005_0001102` | legal content | temporal region | The maximal region throughout which the content is part of the law in force. |
| applies during | `ODP005_0001103` | legal content | temporal region | The maximal region during which the content directly governs what occurs. Asserted only of directly applicable content. |
| consolidates | `ODP005_0001104` | consolidated version | legal content | The version combines the content (base or succeeding). |
| states the law during | `ODP005_0001105` | consolidated version | temporal region | The version's own region, during which it correctly combines the content in force. |
| due within | `ODP005_0001106` | first-order legal position | temporal region | The position is to be discharged within the region. |
| computed from | `ODP005_0001107` | temporal region | occurrent | According to legal content, the region is computed from the occurrent (it need not begin there). |

### Data property

| Label | IRI | Domain | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| has date-time value | `ODP005_0001200` | temporal instant | `xsd:dateTimeStamp` (functional) |

A region's bounds are its BFO instants, related by *has first instant*
(`BFO_0000222`) and *has last instant* (`BFO_0000224`); the date and time sit on
the instant. `xsd:dateTimeStamp` is in the OWL 2 datatype map and requires a
time zone; `xsd:date`, `xsd:duration` and the `xsd:g*` types are not in the map.

**Conventions.** Regions are closed and recorded to the second, so the GDPR's
Directive predecessor is in force until 23:59:59 on 24 May 2018 and the GDPR
applies from 00:00:00 on 25 May. Where the law gives a date and no time, the
time zone is a stated convention, not a rule of the source: for EU acts the
example uses midnight Brussels time. Values are compared in SPARQL after
casting to `xsd:dateTime`.

### Axioms (Manchester syntax)

```
amending act         EquivalentTo  legal act and (amends some legal content)
repeal               EquivalentTo  legal act and (repeals some legal content)
repeals              SubPropertyOf amends
has date-time value  Domain temporal instant, Range xsd:dateTimeStamp, Functional
consolidated version SubClassOf    generically dependent continuant
    and (consolidates some legal content)
    and (states the law during some temporal region)
compliance period    EquivalentTo  temporal region
    and (inverse (due within) some first-order legal position)
```

### Grounding rationale (LOF-P-004)

- **Periods are temporal regions, not processes.** Being in force is not
  something that happens; it is a standing condition that holds through a
  region of time. The pattern therefore relates content to BFO temporal
  regions instead of reifying a "force" entity. Entry into force is the first
  instant of such a region, a BFO temporal instant.
- **Calendar values on instants, not regions.** BFO already relates a region
  to its first and last instants. Putting the date-time on the instant keeps
  one value per entity, makes an instant reusable as the bound of several
  regions (the last instant of a compliance period is also what triggers the
  power to fine), and matches OWL-Time, where `time:inXSDDateTimeStamp` sits
  on `time:Instant`.
- **Amendment as succession of content.** Legal content is a generically
  dependent continuant (ODP-001). Treating amendment as the establishment of
  new content that *succeeds* the old, rather than as a change in old content,
  keeps every version citable as it stood and avoids ascribing change to an
  abstract pattern.
- **Consolidated version as generically dependent continuant.** A
  consolidation is information content about the law. It is not declared
  disjoint from legal content, so that an enacted consolidation can be
  asserted to be both (LOF-P-010). It has its own period, because it stops
  stating the law at the next amendment while the base content stays in force.
- **Compliance period as a defined temporal region.** No new primitive: a
  region is a compliance period by being the period some position is due
  within.

## Example

The GDPR, the Directive it repealed, and the United Kingdom Act that
transposed the Directive, with real dates; and a hypothetical controller that
notifies one breach late and one in time. Excerpt:

```turtle
ex:gdprEnactment a odp1:0001003 ;            # enactment
    odp1:0001100 ex:gdpr ;                   # establishes
    odp5:0001101 ex:directive9546 .          # repeals (Art. 94(1)), with effect from 25 May 2018
ex:dpa1998Enactment a odp1:0001003 ;
    odp1:0001100 ex:dpa1998 ;
    odp1:0001102 ex:directive9546 .          # performed under: transposition

ex:gdpr odp5:0001102 ex:gdprInForce ;        # in force during (Art. 99(1))
        odp5:0001103 ex:gdprApplies ;        # applies during (Art. 99(2))
        odp5:0001108 ex:directive9546 .      # succeeds
ex:directive9546 odp5:0001102 ex:directiveInForce .   # in force, never applying to private processing
ex:dpa1998 odp5:0001102 ex:dpaInForce ; odp5:0001103 ex:dpaInForce .

ex:gdprApplies obo:BFO_0000222 ex:t20180525 .          # has first instant
ex:t20180525 odp5:0001200 "2018-05-24T22:00:00Z"^^xsd:dateTimeStamp .   # 25 May 2018, 00:00 Brussels

ex:window1 odp5:0001107 ex:awareness1 ;      # computed from (aware at 08:40)
    obo:BFO_0000222 ex:window1Start ;        # 09:00: Reg. 1182/71 Art. 3 excludes the starting hour
    obo:BFO_0000224 ex:window1End .          # 5 March 2026, 08:59:59 UTC
ex:notifyDuty1 a odp3:0001002 ;              # duty (Art. 33(1))
    odp5:0001106 ex:window1 ;                # due within
    odp4:0001106 ex:window1 .                # undischarged during
ex:finePower1 a odp3:0001005 ;               # power (Art. 83(4)(a))
    odp4:0001102 ex:window1End .             # triggered by the end of the period
```

Inferred by HermiT, under both ODP-003 options: the adoption of the GDPR is a
*repeal* and so an *amending act*; both seventy-two-hour windows are
*compliance periods*.

| Question | Answer from the example |
|---|---|
| In force on 15 March 2017 | Directive 95/46/EC, the Data Protection Act 1998, and the GDPR |
| Applies to processing in March 2017 | the Data Protection Act 1998; the Directive and the GDPR are in force but do not apply to it |
| Repealed, succeeded, force ended | Directive 95/46/EC, by the adoption of the GDPR, succeeded by the GDPR, end of 24 May 2018 |
| Consolidation | text 02016R0679-20160504 combines the GDPR and states the law from 24 May 2016 |
| Overdue | Acme's duty to notify breach 1, due by 5 March 2026 08:59:59 UTC, computed from its awareness of the breach; the end of the period triggers the authority's power to fine |

Two points the example makes explicit. Article 33(1) requires notification
"without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours"; the
compliance period records the fixed limit, and whether a later notification
with reasons was lawful is for the rule layer. And the fine of Article 83 is a
sanction, not a remedy in the ODP-004 sense of making good the failure, so the
power is linked by what triggers it, not by *remedies* (open question 5).

The example also contains a period that ends before it begins, which the
shapes report as a violation. The full example is
`src/examples/gdpr-timeline.ttl`.

ELI and Akoma Ntoso record these facts about documents and their expressions.
This pattern records them about legal content, so that a period of force can
be related to the positions grounded in that content and to the chain of
ODP-004.

## Consequences and limitations

- **Instance level only.** The pattern records that a particular period was
  computed from a particular occurrence. The rule "seventy-two hours after
  becoming aware" is type-level and belongs to a rule layer, as in ODP-004.
- **No interval reasoning in OWL.** OWL cannot compare date-time values
  within class definitions, so "in force at *t*" and "overdue" are answered by
  SPARQL, and period well-formedness by SHACL. The pattern deliberately does
  not import OWL-Time's interval algebra, which would add Allen relations the
  queries do not need.
- **Suspension and revival** are represented as several *in force during*
  regions for the same content; the gap between them is the suspension.
- **No discharge relation.** The pattern says a position is due within a
  period and, with ODP-004, that it went undischarged during it. It does not
  link a position to the process that discharged it (open question 3).
- **Repeal is not the only end of force.** A sunset clause, implied repeal,
  desuetude, or annulment ends force with no repealing act; the pattern
  records them by the last instant of the region in force alone, so CQ3 sees
  only repeals made by acts.
- **One time line.** The pattern records the periods as now understood. When
  a court annuls content *ex tunc*, the content was in force as people then
  believed and is treated as never in force afterwards. Recording both
  requires a second, transaction-time dimension (open question 6).
- **Jurisdiction.** Nothing in the pattern assumes the European Union. It has
  been checked against the forms below, all of which it can express
  (LOF-P-010):

| Form | How it is expressed |
|---|---|
| Delayed application (GDPR) | *in force during* and *applies during* with different first instants |
| Retroactivity | *applies during* a region that begins before the *in force during* region |
| Sunset clause | an *in force during* region whose last instant is set by the enacting content |
| Repeal with saving provision | *in force during* ends; *applies during* continues for facts before the repeal |
| Suspension, revival | two *in force during* regions |
| Directive and transposition | the directive *in force during* only; the transposing content *applies during*, its enactment *performed under* the directive (ODP-001) |
| Positive-law codification (US Code) | a consolidated version asserted also to be legal content established by an enactment |
| Annulment by a court | the last instant of the region in force, with no repealing act |

## Open questions for the founding coordinators

1. **Amendment target.** *Amends* relates an act to the content it amends,
   following ODP-001, and *succeeds* relates the texts. Should *amended by*
   (content to act) also be provided, or is the inverse enough?
2. **Limitation periods and other periods for second-order positions.** A
   limitation period, or a time to appeal, is a period within which a power
   may be exercised, not one within which a position must be discharged.
   *Due within* does not cover it. Should there be a parallel *exercisable
   within* for powers, here or in ODP-004?
3. **Discharge.** Should a relation from a position to the process that
   discharged it (*discharged by*) be added here, in ODP-004, or in a
   compliance pattern of its own?
4. **Versions of positions.** When content is succeeded, are the positions it
   grounds ended and replaced, or do they persist? Under ODP-003 Option A
   they are replaced; under Option B they may persist. The pattern takes no
   position, and the example does not depend on it.
5. **Sanctions.** A fine is not a remedy in the ODP-004 sense. Should ODP-004
   distinguish *sanctions* (powers whose exercise penalizes the failure) from
   *remedies* (positions that make it good), with sanctioning positions linked
   to the failure they penalize?
6. **Bitemporality.** Annulment *ex tunc* (and the Akoma Ntoso treatment of
   unconstitutionality) needs a record of what was believed in force at a
   time as well as what is now held to have been in force. Should the
   Foundry add a transaction-time layer, or leave it to the data store?
7. **Instants as triggers.** The end of a compliance period triggers the power
   to fine. ODP-004 *triggered by* has the range occurrent, and a BFO instant
   is a temporal region, an occurrent, so this is valid; should ODP-004 say so
   explicitly?

## Related patterns

- [LOF-ODP-001](odp-001.md) supplies *legal act*, *legal content*,
  *enactment*, and *performed under*; amending acts and repeals are legal acts.
- [LOF-ODP-002](odp-002.md) is imported with the other base patterns. The
  pattern uses none of its terms directly, but content that confers a role
  (an office with a fixed term) is given its periods like any other.
- [LOF-ODP-003](odp-003.md) supplies *first-order legal position*, the domain
  of *due within*.
- [LOF-ODP-004](odp-004.md) supplies *triggered by* and *undischarged
  during*; entry into force and the end of a compliance period are typical
  triggers.
- [LOF-ODP-006](odp-006.md) uses these periods for the scope in time of a
  derogation; lex posterior compares enactments (ODP-001).
