# Legal Ontologies Foundry

The **Legal Ontologies Foundry (LOF)** is a collaborative initiative to develop
interoperable, logically well-formed ontologies for the legal domain.
Following the model of the [OBO Foundry](https://obofoundry.org/), member
ontologies share a common upper ontology
([Basic Formal Ontology](https://basic-formal-ontology.org/), ISO/IEC 21838-2),
a common format (OWL 2), persistent identifiers, and a common set of
[principles](principles/index.md). As a result, models of obligations, rights,
roles, institutions, legal acts, and evidence built by different groups can be
combined without translation loss.

!!! note "Founding draft"
    This site is in its founding phase. The principles, policies, and
    governance pages are drafts for ratification by the founding coordinators.
    Proposed changes are welcome as pull requests or in
    [Discussions](https://github.com/legal-ontologies-foundry/legal-ontologies-foundry.github.io/discussions).

## Why a foundry

Legal information systems, from court records and statutory databases to
compliance tools and AI systems that reason about law, increasingly depend on
machine-readable representations of legal entities. Existing legal vocabularies
were developed largely in isolation, with incompatible upper-level commitments,
which makes them difficult to integrate or audit. The Foundry applies the
methods that have succeeded in the biomedical sciences: shared principles,
coordinated development, public review, and orthogonal ontologies that each
cover a well-delimited domain.
