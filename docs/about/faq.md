# Frequently asked questions

## How does the Foundry relate to the OBO Foundry?

The Legal Ontologies Foundry adopts the OBO Foundry's methods: shared
principles, a common upper ontology (BFO), persistent identifiers, a public
registry, and coordinated review. It applies them to the legal domain, which
OBO does not cover. See [related initiatives](related-initiatives.md).

## Do I have to accept a particular theory of law to contribute?

No. The Foundry requires that legal entities be grounded in BFO and that the
grounding be explicit and argued (LOF-P-004). It does not require any
particular jurisprudential theory. Competing groundings can be documented and
compared through the design pattern series.

## Can an ontology cover only one jurisdiction?

Yes. Many will. LOF-P-010 requires only that jurisdiction-specific content be
labeled as such.

## Why w3id.org rather than our own domain?

w3id.org is a community-maintained, permanent redirect service. It lets the
Foundry move its hosting without breaking any identifier. See
[identifiers](../policy/identifiers.md).

## Can I use Foundry ontologies commercially?

Yes. All Foundry ontologies are released under CC BY 4.0 or CC0 (LOF-P-001).

## How do I propose a new ontology?

Follow the [new ontology request SOP](../sop/new-ontology-request.md).
