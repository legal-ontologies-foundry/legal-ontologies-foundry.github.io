    Full text

    Raised at the 21 October meeting (Bill, on Reinach's promise; see the layer schema slides, version 2, slide 6).

    The point. For Reinach, a promise is a social act that must be taken up (vernommen) by the person it is addressed to. Without uptake, no claim and no obligation arise. Bill's diagram of a promise between John and Sue shows:

    • the act of promising, in which both take part;
    • the promisor and promisee roles;
    • a claim borne by Sue and an obligation borne by John, each dependent on the other.

    This matches the 0.2.0 patterns closely:

    In the diagram In the patterns
    claim and obligation, each dependent on the other ODP-003 correlative positions (Option A: positions inhere in their holders)
    promisor and promisee roles ODP-002 roles brought about by the act
    the words on a napkin ODP-001 performed by means of, or evidences (#8)

    What the patterns lack is uptake.

    Proposal for discussion. Uptake is not normative reasoning. It is the addressee's grasping of the act: in Mental Functioning Ontology terms, a cognitive process. So it belongs in ODP-002 and ODP-001, not in the Normative Reasoning Ontology:

    • an uptake is a process in which the addressee of a social act apprehends it;
    • a social act addressed to someone (a promise, a command, a notice) has part some uptake by that addressee, or is completed only when one occurs;
    • the claim and obligation (ODP-003) arise from the act with its uptake.

    Questions.

    1. Is uptake a part of the act, or a condition of its legal effect? The latter fits acts that take effect on receipt, such as notices, or on publication.
    2. Which acts need uptake? Promising and commanding do. Enactment, publication, and a will plausibly do not.
    3. Software parties. When contracts are formed or accepted by software, uptake cannot require a brain. This is the same issue as #40: uptake should be a process with a cognitive kind, not only a mental one.

    Raised by the "LOF Layer Schema" slides (21 October 2026), which propose the Foundry's layer structure and a Normative Reasoning Ontology (NRO), compared with the Normative Accountability Ontology (NAO). Declaration of interest: NAO is David Koepsell's own ontology.

    The NRO models the forward-looking half of normative life: an agent working out what ought to be done. NAO models the backward-looking half: a norm is breached, the breach leaves something behind, and someone answers for it. Proposed links:

    • Capacity. NAO's accountable agent bears some normative reasoning capacity: a disposition realized in normative reasoning processes. This gives NAO's standing and counterfactual-control conditions a cognitive basis. Tested. A draft bridge between the NRO, NAO, LOF-ODP-001 to 004, the Common Core Ontologies, and the Mental Functioning Ontology was checked with HermiT; results are quoted below. The files will be posted once the Foundry settles where bridge files live. In NAO's Reset Button example, the human (Reyes) and the permanent AI (UNITY) are both classified as normative reasoners, and the resettable systems are not. This is coherent only with the change proposed in the issue on MFO mental processes.
    • Judgment and breach. An NRO normative judgment that some conduct breached a norm is about a NAO normative breach. Accountability responses (apology, restitution, sanction) follow such a judgment, but the breach does not depend on it.
    • Interpretation. The NRO's norm interpretation process answers the question left open in #31: how to record that someone interpreted a provision as expressing a given norm. The interpretation is a process, with the interpreter as participant, the provision as input, and the norm as output.
    • Conflict. The NRO's norm conflict resolution process is where a typology of normative contradictions fits, for example the one in A Structural Ontology of the Law. The NRO names the process; the typology classifies the conflicts it resolves. In the bridge it requires at least two norms as input.

    Scope¶

    What it is to be held to account:

    • a norm is breached, and the breach is someone's conduct;
    • the breach leaves something behind in those it connects;
    • someone answers for it, through apology, restitution, sanction, or other responses.

    NAO covers legal, moral, professional, and institutional norms. One distinction is central: between what can be reset and what carries its own record. That distinction is what lets NAO say who answers when an AI system or other artifact does wrong.

    What exists today (version 0.1.0, draft)¶

    NAO is complete and tested, not a plan. It has five patterns:

    Pattern Covers
    P1 norm, breach, attribution
    P2 residue and record
    P3 capacities and answerability
    P4 responses
    P5 the Irreversible Accountability Test

    It is published as two modules:

    • NAO core: 23 classes and 12 relations, with no commitments beyond BFO 2020 and LOF-ODP-001 to 004.
    • NAO-IAT: the Irreversible Accountability Test as two exclusion axioms, kept apart so the vocabulary can be adopted without the thesis.

    It comes with:

    • A worked example of three scenarios from the classroom demonstration The Reset Button: a physician, a resettable diagnostic system, a permanent AI, and an autonomous probe. A plain-language report states who answers for what. It also finds an accountability gap: a breach no one answers for.
    • Checks: HermiT consistency, SPARQL quality checks, a ROBOT report, SHACL shapes with readable messages, and three thesis tests. Each test is inconsistent with the IAT module and consistent without it. All of these run in CI.

    Competency questions¶

    1. Which norm did this conduct breach, whose conduct was it, and whom did it wrong?
    2. Is this breach a repeat of an earlier one?
    3. What does a breach leave behind, and in whom? What records exist about it, and can they be changed?
    4. Who answers for this breach, and through which responses?
    5. Is answerability for an artifact's breach displaced to whoever deploys it? Which breaches does no one answer for?
    6. Which agents satisfy the five conditions of the IAT, and which conditions do the others fail?
    7. Is a moral answerability codified as a legal position, and does the breach trigger it (LOF-ODP-003, ODP-004)?

    Fit with the Foundry¶

    • Layer. NAO belongs in the general normative layer proposed in #38, beside the Normative Reasoning Ontology. It covers what happens after a breach; the NRO covers reasoning before one (#43).
    • Norm. NAO's norm is the broad class proposed as the hub in #39. LOF-ODP-001 legal norm sits under it, and both sit under the CCO prescriptive information content entity.
    • Legal patterns. NAO imports LOF-ODP-001 to 004. A breach that triggers a codified legal position is inferred to be an ODP-004 triggering occurrence.
    • Tested with the NRO. In the NRO and NAO bridge (#40, #43), NAO's thesis module and worked example stay consistent. The human and the permanent AI are both classified as normative reasoners; the resettable systems are not.

    Overlap check (LOF-P-009)¶

    Topic Who covers it
    Legal remedies ODP-004 remedial position. NAO does not redefine it.
    NAO responses (apology, restitution, sanction) NAO: processes that realize answerability. They may realize a remedial position but are not one.
    Legal positions ODP-003. NAO's codified as points into ODP-003 rather than duplicating it.
    Breach by omission ODP-004 undischarged during. NAO's open question 4 proposes adopting it.

    Decisions for the Foundry¶

    1. Register at draft status. This is a routine change under the Governance page, once the scope discussion here is settled. The registry entry is ready in the linked pull request.
    2. Repository. Host NAO in the organization as lof-nao (the SOP's default), or link the author's own repository.
    3. Identifiers (LOF-P-005). NAO's IRIs are currently https://sool.davidkoepsell.com/nao/NAO_nnnnnnn. P-005 requires https://w3id.org/lof/NAO_nnnnnnn. NAO is a draft no one else yet depends on, so migrating now is cheap: new IRIs in 0.2.0, with each old IRI deprecated and pointing to its replacement.
    4. The thesis module. Should NAO-IAT be registered with the core, as an optional module, or kept outside the Foundry as the author's own theory? Keeping it apart is NAO's own design, so either works.
    5. Open questions to settle in review. These come from NAO's specification:
    6. the BFO category of moral residue;
    7. the category of normative standing;
    8. a weaker, time-indexed version of thesis 2;
    9. breach by omission;
    10. whether accountability gaps should be validation errors.

    Raised by the "LOF Layer Schema" slides (21 October 2026), which propose the Foundry's layer structure and a Normative Reasoning Ontology (NRO), compared with the Normative Accountability Ontology (NAO). Declaration of interest: NAO is David Koepsell's own ontology.

    The point. The schema places the Normative Reasoning Ontology in the domain layer, beneath the Legal Core Ontology. But the NRO covers moral, military, organizational, and regulatory reasoning, not only legal reasoning. NAO likewise covers moral and professional norms as well as legal ones. Neither is a specialization of a legal core.

    Proposal. Add a general normative layer between the reference layer and the Legal Core, holding what is common to all norms:

    • norms;
    • breaches;
    • normative reasoning (NRO);
    • accountability (NAO).

    The Legal Core would then specialize it: a legal norm is a kind of norm, and legal reasoning is a kind of normative reasoning. This is how LOF-ODP-001 and NAO already relate: NAO's norm includes legal content, and moral or institutional norms that are not legal content.

    A small correction to the diagram. The Document Acts ontology is drawn as a module of the Common Core Ontologies. Its terms are in the IAO namespace (IAO_00210xx, as noted in #10 and #11), and it is released separately. It may be clearer to show it as a separate import alongside the CCO.

    Full text

    Raised by the "LOF Layer Schema" slides (21 October 2026), which propose the Foundry's layer structure and a Normative Reasoning Ontology (NRO), compared with the Normative Accountability Ontology (NAO). Declaration of interest: NAO is David Koepsell's own ontology.

    The problem. The slides name autonomous decision-support systems as a main use of the NRO. But they place normative reasoning process under MFO thinking with language, and so under mental process. The Mental Functioning Ontology defines a mental process as "a bodily process that occurs in the brain". Every bodily process has some organism as participant at all times (OGMS_0000060: has participant at all times some extended organism). As proposed, an artifact cannot engage in normative reasoning at all.

    Tested. A draft bridge between the NRO, NAO, LOF-ODP-001 to 004, the Common Core Ontologies, and the Mental Functioning Ontology was checked with HermiT; results are quoted below. The files will be posted once the Foundry settles where bridge files live.

    Case NRO as proposed NRO revised
    A decision-support system (NAO's DiagnoBot) evaluates whether an action is permitted inconsistent consistent: a normative reasoning process, not a mental one
    A judge reasons, thinking with language consistent consistent: inferred to be a mental normative reasoning process

    Proposal.

    • Make normative reasoning process a BFO process.
    • Define mental normative reasoning process as normative reasoning process and MFO mental process.
    • Add computational normative reasoning process for artifacts.

    NAO takes the same line: what makes an entity answerable is a disposition, not a substrate.

    Also noted. The MFO uses BFO 2 temporalized relations: has participant at all times (BFO_0000167) and inheres in at all times (BFO_0000052). A stack built on BFO 2020 needs these mapped to BFO_0000057 and BFO_0000197. The bridge does this.

    Raised by the "LOF Layer Schema" slides (21 October 2026), which propose the Foundry's layer structure and a Normative Reasoning Ontology (NRO), compared with the Normative Accountability Ontology (NAO). Declaration of interest: NAO is David Koepsell's own ontology.

    The problem. Once the schema's layers are combined, there are five near-duplicate classes for a norm:

    Ontology Class Placement
    CCO Prescriptive Information Content Entity (ont00000965) information content entity
    NRO (slides) stated / documented norm information content entity
    NAO norm (NAO_0001022) generically dependent continuant
    LOF-ODP-001 legal norm (ODP001_0001005) legal content
    D-Acts deontic classes information content entity

    Proposal. Make the CCO prescriptive information content entity the hub, and place the others relative to it:

    • CCO prescriptive information content entity, the hub;
    • NAO norm, the broad class: anything that prescribes, permits, or prohibits conduct, including unwritten moral and customary norms;
      • LOF-ODP-001 legal norm, already placed under CCO process regulation by the LOF and CCO bridge;
    • NRO stated norm, a defined class: a norm that some material entity, such as a document, is a carrier of.

    Tested. A draft bridge between the NRO, NAO, LOF-ODP-001 to 004, the Common Core Ontologies, and the Mental Functioning Ontology was checked with HermiT; results are quoted below. The files will be posted once the Foundry settles where bridge files live. With these axioms, a regulatory provision carried by an Official Gazette is inferred to be a stated norm, a NAO norm, a legal norm, and a CCO prescriptive ICE. The whole is consistent.

    Open question for the CCO community. Is an unwritten norm, held only in minds and practices, a CCO information content entity? NAO assumes so; the CCO treatment of concretization may not.

    Raised by the "LOF Layer Schema" slides (21 October 2026), which propose the Foundry's layer structure and a Normative Reasoning Ontology (NRO), compared with the Normative Accountability Ontology (NAO). Declaration of interest: NAO is David Koepsell's own ontology.

    1. Two axes of classification. Slide 3 divides normative reasoning by domain: legal, moral, regulatory, organizational. Slide 5 adds a second division, by task: applicability, interpretation, obligation, permission and prohibition assessment, conflict resolution, justification, judgment. Asserting both gives multiple inheritance, which the BFO and OBO conventions avoid. Regulatory reasoning is also defined as "a legal or organizational normative reasoning process", overlapping its own siblings.

    Proposal.

    • Assert the task axis.
    • Define the domain classes by the kind of norm that is an input. For example, legal reasoning process is a normative reasoning process that has as input some legal norm.
    • Make regulatory norm a kind of legal norm. Regulatory reasoning is then inferred to be a kind of legal reasoning, with no overlap to assert.

    Tested. A draft bridge between the NRO, NAO, LOF-ODP-001 to 004, the Common Core Ontologies, and the Mental Functioning Ontology was checked with HermiT; results are quoted below. The files will be posted once the Foundry settles where bridge files live. The inference holds. An obligation assessment whose input is a regulatory norm is classified as legal reasoning, regulatory reasoning, and obligation assessment.

    2. Relations. The slides' "input" is not a BFO relation. Proposal: use has input and has output from the Relation Ontology (RO_0002233, RO_0002234).

    3. Judgment. Normative judgment process is defined as "an act of judging", not in the genus-and-difference form of its siblings. Proposal:

    • a normative judgment process is a normative reasoning process whose output is a normative judgment;
    • a normative judgment is CCO descriptive information content;
    • a breach finding is a judgment that is about some normative breach (NAO).

    The finding does not create the breach. The breach exists whether or not anyone judges it.

    Raised by Danilo Delôgo Tavares's proposal "What a legal ontology must tell apart" (Anatel), a walk-through of the Brazilian legal order from the Constitution to an appeal. Slides 8 and 9.

    The gap. None of the four patterns says when legal content is in force. Danilo's Anatel Resolution 765/2023 example separates, each with its own date:

    • the act (the Board's vote, 26 Oct 2023) and the signing (6 Nov 2023);
    • publication (10 Nov 2023), and two corrective republications, which under LINDB Art. 1 § 3 restart the waiting period;
    • entry into force: some articles at once, the rest postponed by a later Board decision to 1 Sep 2025;
    • revocation of the previous regulation on that date, and in Oct 2026 the partial revocation of Arts. 42 to 85 only.

    Validity is an interval, opened and closed by other acts, and it can differ article by article.

    Proposal. A new pattern (LOF-ODP-005), covering:

    • an in force during relation between legal content and a temporal region, at the level of single provisions;
    • publication as an occurrent distinct from the act that establishes the content;
    • acts whose object is another text's validity: postponement, repeal, revocation, and amendment;
    • point-in-time versions, aligned with Akoma Ntoso and ELI rather than invented.

    Questions.

    1. Is publication a legal act, or an occurrent that conditions the effect of one?
    2. Does a repealed provision cease to exist, or continue to exist but stop being in force? This matters for LINDB Art. 6; see the companion issue on vested rights.
    3. How should content that lapses with no terminating act be handled, such as a provisional measure not converted within 60 + 60 days (Constitution Art. 62)?

    Full text

    Raised by Danilo Delôgo Tavares's proposal "What a legal ontology must tell apart" (Anatel), a walk-through of the Brazilian legal order from the Constitution to an appeal. Slides 3, 4, 10 and 11.

    The gap. ODP-001 makes legal norm a subclass of legal content, so the wording of an article and the norm it expresses are the same kind of thing. The deck argues, following Humberto Ávila, that text is not norm: one article can contain several norms, and one norm can draw on several articles. The passage from one to the other is interpretation, which is where the hardest questions lie (slide 19).

    Proposal.

    • Distinguish a legal provision (a unit of text: an article, a paragraph, an item, a clause) from a legal norm (what it requires, prohibits, or permits). Both are information content. Relate them by an expresses relation that is many-to-many.
    • Model the parthood of text units fixed by statute. In Brazil, Complementary Law 95/1998 Art. 10 sets out the hierarchy: Part > Book > Title > Chapter > Section > Subsection > Article, and within an article, caput > inciso > alínea > item, plus parágrafo. Headings are already covered by ODP-001's legal designator: they group and name, and only articles carry norms.
    • Do not invent the structural vocabulary. Align with Akoma Ntoso, and with LexML Brasil, Brazil's standard for legal identifiers and markup, so that each unit can carry a persistent identifier under LOF-P-005.

    Questions.

    1. Is a norm a separate entity, or should the LOF model only provisions and the positions they ground (ODP-003), leaving norms implicit?
    2. How should the LOF record an interpretation, that is, a claim that a provision expresses a given norm, made by someone, at some time?

    Full text

    Raised by Danilo Delôgo Tavares's proposal "What a legal ontology must tell apart" (Anatel), a walk-through of the Brazilian legal order from the Constitution to an appeal. Slides 15, 16 and 17.

    The gap. ODP-001 defines legal content as content established by some legal act. Several kinds of content in legal documents are not established in that sense:

    • Allegations. A complaint and a defence make contrary claims about the same event. The ontology must keep "the document says that X" apart from "X happened".
    • The report and reasoning of a judgment. Under the Brazilian Code of Civil Procedure, Arts. 489 and 503, only the operative part (dispositivo) has the force of law. The report describes and the reasoning argues.
    • A requested decision. An appeal argues for a decision that was never issued. Bills, draft regulations, and this repository's own proposed changes are the same kind of thing.

    Proposal.

    • Add descriptive and argumentative content related to its subject by is about. The CCO bridge in 0.2.0 already imports IAO is about and the CCO descriptive and directive information content classes.
    • Add a class for proposed legal content: directive content that some act asks to be established, but that has not been.

    Note. Filings create no norms, but they are still legal acts with legal effects. They generate procedural positions: the court's duty to decide, the defendant's burden to answer, and the presumption that uncontested facts are true (CPC Art. 341). These fit ODP-004 as acts that generate positions.

    Full text

    Raised by Danilo Delôgo Tavares's proposal "What a legal ontology must tell apart" (Anatel), a walk-through of the Brazilian legal order from the Constitution to an appeal. Slide 9.

    The question. LINDB Art. 6 says that a new law has immediate and general effect, but respects:

    • the consummated legal act: one completed under the law in force when it was performed;
    • the vested right: a right its holder may already exercise;
    • res judicata: a decision no longer open to appeal.

    Under ODP-003 (Option A, adopted in 0.2.0), a legal position is a role grounded in legal content. If that content is repealed, does a vested right lapse? The law says it does not.

    Two readings to choose between.

    1. grounded in records grounding at the time the position came into existence, and the position's continued existence does not depend on the content staying in force; or
    2. the position persists because some other content, here LINDB Art. 6 itself, grounds its survival.

    A Hohfeldian account. A vested right, and res judicata, can be modeled as an immunity of the holder, correlative to a disability of the legislature or the courts to alter the position. That is exactly what ODP-003's second-order positions are for. It would make a good worked example for them.

    Suggested competency question. Which positions held by X remain in force after content C is repealed, and on what ground?

    Raised by Danilo Delôgo Tavares's proposal "What a legal ontology must tell apart" (Anatel), a walk-through of the Brazilian legal order from the Constitution to an appeal. Slides 13 and 20.

    1. More than one ground. In the deck's mobile service contract, clause 9 ("the consumer may terminate at any time, without charge") restates Anatel's consumer rights regulation, Art. 4, XVI. The consumer's position is then grounded both in the regulation and in the contract. ODP-003 should say explicitly that grounded in may have more than one value, and whether the two grounds differ in kind: the regulation grounds the position, while the contract binds it to named parties.

    2. Joint and several liability. Under the Consumer Protection Code (Art. 7, sole paragraph, and Art. 18), every supplier in the chain is jointly and severally liable to the consumer. ODP-003 defines correlative of as a symmetric relation between two positions, "each of which exists if and only if the other does". One claim matched by the duties of several suppliers does not fit that one-to-one definition. The options:

    • one claim per debtor, each with its own correlative duty, plus an axiom that discharge by one discharges all; or
    • a correlative relation that is one-to-many.

    3. A note on the termination right. The deck pairs "Maria's right to terminate" with "the provider's duty to stop billing". In Hohfeld's terms the right to terminate is a power, correlative to the provider's liability. The duty to stop billing comes into existence only when the power is exercised, through ODP-003 exercised in and ODP-004 generated by. This could be the pattern's worked example of the difference between first-order and second-order positions.
