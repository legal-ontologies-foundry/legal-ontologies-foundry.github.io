    LOF-ODP-006: Relations between Norms¶
    Identifier LOF-ODP-006
    IRI https://w3id.org/lof/odp-006.owl
    Repository https://github.com/legal-ontologies-foundry/lof-odp-006
    Status draft
    Version 0.1.0
    Depends on LOF-ODP-001, LOF-ODP-003
    Drafted by David R. Koepsell
    For review by William Mandrick, Barry Smith

    Draft for review

    First draft, prepared for discussion by the founding coordinators. Open questions are listed at the end.

    Intent¶

    Legal content rarely stands alone. A national statute fills in what a regulation leaves open; a research exemption, permitted by the regulation itself, removes cases from a right of access; a specific consent rule displaces a general legal basis; a municipal ordinance collides with a regulation of higher rank. To answer what the law requires of someone, one has to know which content bears on which, which items conflict, and which prevails, on what ground, and on whose authority.

    This pattern provides those relations. It treats conflict as a relation between the legal positions two items of content ground, which is where incompatibility actually lies, and infers conflict between the content from it. It treats a statement that one item prevails over another as an entity in its own right, because it has three parts that matter (which prevails, which yields, on what ground) and a source whose authority may differ: a conflict rule in the legislation, a court's holding, or guidance with no legal force. The pattern keeps those apart: any determination makes one content held to prevail over another, but only a determination that is itself legal content makes it prevail over it. And it keeps open the cases the law leaves open: a conflict that nothing resolves, and one on which authorities disagree, are reported, not hidden.

    Competency questions¶

    1. Which provisions particularize, derogate from, or create exceptions to which, and under what content was the act containing them enacted?
    2. Which items of legal content conflict, through which positions, held by whom?
    3. For each determination, which content prevails, on what ground, according to whom, and does the determination have legal force?
    4. What is the status of each conflict: settled with legal force, held only by readings without force, contested, or unresolved?

    All four are answered by SPARQL (src/sparql/); unresolved, contested, and unauthoritatively resolved conflicts are also reported by the SHACL shapes (src/shapes/).

    Classes¶

    Label IRI BFO parent Definition
    priority principle ODP006_0001000 generically dependent continuant A generically dependent continuant that states, on some ground such as rank, specificity, or time, which of two conflicting items of legal content prevails.
    precedence determination ODP006_0001001 generically dependent continuant A generically dependent continuant that states that one item of legal content prevails over another where they conflict.
    authoritative precedence determination ODP006_0001002 precedence determination (defined) A precedence determination that is itself legal content.

    Four priority principles are provided as individuals: lex superior (ODP006_0001500), lex specialis (ODP006_0001501), lex posterior (ODP006_0001502), and primacy of an overriding legal order (ODP006_0001503). The last covers any regime in which the content of one legal order prevails over conflicting content of another: European Union law over national law (Case 6/64 Costa v ENEL), federal law over state law in the United States (Constitution Article VI, clause 2), federal paramountcy in Canada, section 109 of the Australian Constitution, and the Charter of the United Nations over other treaty obligations (Article 103). It is kept apart from lex superior, which ranks content within a single legal order, because it relates two orders, and its effect on the yielding content varies by regime: set aside in the case under EU law (Case 106/77 Simmenthal), without effect or inoperative under US preemption and Canadian paramountcy, invalid to the extent of the inconsistency in Australia. The pattern does not fix that effect (see open question 1). A regime-specific variant that needs its own identity can be added as a further individual with dcterms:coverage naming the regime. Lex posterior compares the enactments that established the content (ODP-001), not their dates of entry into force; where a system uses another date, record the choice.

    The first chain lifts conflict from positions to the content that grounds them. The second makes held to prevail over the shadow of every determination. The last three do the same for prevails over, but only for determinations asserted to be legal content: the self-link, which holds of an individual exactly when it is an authoritative determination, carries the class into the property chain. Authoritatively favours is not declared a subproperty of favours, because a chain-defined property cannot be a subproperty of a property in its own chain without making the hierarchy irregular, which OWL 2 DL forbids.

    Derogation does not imply precedence. An earlier draft made derogates from a subproperty of prevails over. That made BDSG section 27(2) prevail over the GDPR, which is wrong: the German provision restricts the right of access only because GDPR Article 89(2) permits it, and if the two truly conflicted, the GDPR would prevail. The authorization is now recorded with ODP-001: the enactment of the derogating content is performed under the content that permits it.

    Grounding rationale (LOF-P-004)¶

    • Conflict lies between positions. Two texts are not incompatible as texts; they are incompatible because no one can comply with both, or because one permits what the other forbids. That is a fact about the positions they ground (ODP-003), held by particular parties. Locating conflict there makes it precise (whose positions, held by whom) and makes the content-level relation derivable rather than stipulated.
    • Determinations are information content. That one provision prevails over another is stated somewhere and can be stated differently elsewhere. It is therefore a generically dependent continuant, for the reason legal content is (ODP-001): the same determination is borne by a judgment, a commentary, and a database record. The act of determining is a process; the determination is what it yields. It is asserted to be legal content as well when its source makes it so (a conflict rule in a statute, a court's holding), and is then classified as authoritative.
    • Force is a property of the determination, not of the relation. Whether A prevails over B in law depends on who said so. Deriving prevails over from authoritative determinations only, and held to prevail over from all of them, lets guidance, scholarship, and annotation be recorded without being mistaken for law.
    • Priority principles are not declared legal content. Lex specialis and lex posterior are codified in some systems (for example the Italian preleggi) and doctrinal in others. Declaring them legal content would build one tradition into the pattern (LOF-P-010).
    • No new primitives for derogation. Derogation and specification are relations between items of ODP-001 legal content. Their scope (which cases) is type-level and is left to the rule layer.

    Example¶

    From the GDPR's neighbourhood, with real provisions and a hypothetical shop, municipality, and court. Excerpt:

    ex:bdsgEnactment a odp1:0001003 ;               # enactment of the BDSG 2018
    odp1:0001100 ex:bdsg26_2 , ex:bdsg27_2 ;    # establishes
    odp1:0001102 ex:gdprArt88 , ex:gdprArt89_2 .  # performed under
ex:bdsg26_2 odp6:0001100 ex:gdprArt7 .           # s. 26(2) particularizes Art. 7 (consent in employment)
ex:bdsg27_2 odp6:0001103 ex:gdprArt15 .          # s. 27(2) creates exception to Art. 15

ex:consentDuty a odp3:0001002 ;                  # duty, grounded in TDDDG s. 25 and ePrivacy Art. 5(3)
    odp3:0001101 ex:tdddg25 , ex:eprivacy5_3 .
ex:interestLiberty a odp3:0001003 ;              # liberty (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f))
    odp3:0001101 ex:gdprArt6_1f ;
    odp6:0001104 ex:consentDuty .                # conflicts with

ex:recitalDetermination a odp6:0001001 ;         # not legal content: recitals do not bind (C-162/97 Nilsson)
    odp6:0001106 ex:eprivacy5_3 ; odp6:0001107 ex:gdprArt6_1f ;
    odp6:0001108 odp6:0001501 ;                  # lex specialis
    odp6:0001110 ex:gdprRecital173 .

ex:courtDetermination a odp6:0001001 , odp1:0001001 ;   # a holding: legal content
    odp6:0001106 ex:gdprArt9_1 ; odp6:0001107 ex:ordinance ;
    odp6:0001108 odp6:0001503 ;                  # primacy of an overriding legal order (EU law)
    odp6:0001110 ex:judgment .

    Inferred by HermiT, under both ODP-003 options:

    Content Inferred relation Content From
    ePrivacy Art. 5(3); TDDDG s. 25 content conflicts with, held to prevail over GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) the conflicting positions; Recital 173 and EDPB Opinion 5/2019, lex specialis, neither with legal force
    GDPR Art. 9(1) content conflicts with, held to prevail over, prevails over municipal ordinance (hypothetical) the conflicting duties; an annotator's reading and a court's holding (hypothetical), primacy of an overriding legal order (EU law)
    BDSG s. 27(2) creates exception to, and nothing more GDPR Art. 15 the derogation; the BDSG enactment is performed under Art. 89(2)

    The court's determination is classified as an authoritative precedence determination because it is asserted to be legal content; the annotator's, Recital 173's, and the EDPB's are not.

    Section 26(2) is used rather than section 26(1) because the validity of 26(1) as an Article 88 rule is doubtful after Case C-34/21 (Hauptpersonalrat, 30 March 2023), which held that a national rule merely restating the GDPR is not one.

    The example also contains two deliberate defects. Provisions A and B ground conflicting duties and nothing resolves them: the shapes report an unresolved conflict (a warning). Two readings disagree on whether C or D prevails: the shapes report contested precedence (a warning), and CQ4 reports the conflict as contested. The cookie conflict is reported as resolved only by determinations without legal force (information). The full example is src/examples/gdpr-norm-relations.ttl.

    Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)¶

    The mappings are also published as SSSOM in src/mappings/odp-006-standards.sssom.tsv.

    LegalRuleML expresses defeasible priority among rules for a reasoner, at the type level. This pattern records, at the instance level, that a particular conflict exists and how and by whom it was resolved. A LegalRuleML rule base can be generated from the determinations, and its conclusions recorded back as determinations.

    Consequences and limitations¶

    • Conflict is asserted. Whether two positions conflict depends on the conduct they concern, which is the action-type problem of ODP-003 open question 2. Until that is solved, conflict between positions is asserted by the annotator, and only conflict between content is inferred.
    • No scope. Derogation and specification hold "for the cases within its scope". The scope is not represented, so the pattern cannot say for which facts the derogating content governs. That belongs to the rule layer.
    • Open world. OWL can infer that a conflict is resolved but not that it is unresolved, since a determination may simply be unrecorded. Unresolved conflicts are reported by SHACL on a reasoned graph.
    • No asymmetry axiom, and cycles are not errors. OWL 2 DL forbids declaring a property defined by a chain asymmetric, so mutual precedence is not an inconsistency. It is reported by SHACL as contested (a warning), because it can be the law's real state: courts of two legal orders can each hold, with authority in their own order, that their law prevails (compare the Court of Justice and the German Federal Constitutional Court in the PSPP case, 2 BvR 859/15, 5 May 2020).
    • Not transitive. Precedence is not transitive across different scopes (A over B for employees, B over C for contractors says nothing of A and C), so neither precedence relation is declared transitive.
    • Other precedence regimes. Federal preemption (including field preemption), UN Charter Article 103, Article 30 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, and precedence clauses in contracts all have the shape of a priority principle; they are not provided as individuals, and can be added with their own coverage.
    • Lex posterior needs dates. The pattern does not compare dates. With LOF-ODP-001 enactments placed in time by LOF-ODP-005, a SPARQL check can confirm that content favoured on the ground of lex posterior was enacted after the content it displaces.

    Relation to A Structural Ontology of the Law¶

    The typology of legal contradictions in Koepsell, A Structural Ontology of the Law (Palgrave Macmillan, forthcoming) classifies conflicts by the link of the Minimum Legal Chain at which they arise. This pattern supplies only what such a typology needs from the Foundry: conflicts located at legal positions, with their content, holders, and resolution status. The typology itself is theory-specific and is left out, consistent with LOF-P-008 and LOF-P-009, as in ODP-004.

    Declaration of interest. The drafter is the author of that book.

    Open questions for the founding coordinators¶

    1. What "prevails" means. The yielding content may be invalid (lex superior within one order), set aside or inoperative in the case (primacy of an overriding legal order, as with EU law), or merely displaced for the cases in scope (lex specialis). Should prevails over carry the effect, for example by subproperties, or should the effect be read from the priority principle?
    2. Conflict between positions of different holders. A duty of A to do X and a claim of B that A not do X are a conflict, though held by different parties. Should the pattern define the kinds of conflict (same holder, correlative pairs) or leave conflicts with general, as now?
    3. Who makes a determination authoritative. The pattern treats a determination as authoritative when it is asserted to be legal content. Should authority instead be relative to a legal order, so that the PSPP case is two authoritative holdings each with force in its own order?
    4. Priority among principles. Lex specialis and lex posterior can point in opposite directions (a later general rule against an earlier special one). Should the pattern represent second-order priority between principles, or leave that to the rule layer?
    5. Particularization and validity. The line between making content more determinate and displacing it is contested in EU law (Article 88 after C-34/21). Should particularizes carry a note that it does not certify the particularizing content's validity?

    Related patterns¶

    • LOF-ODP-001 supplies legal content, between which all norm relations hold, and performed under, which records the content that authorizes a derogation.
    • LOF-ODP-003 supplies the legal positions between which conflict holds, and grounded in, through which it is lifted to content.
    • LOF-ODP-004 supplies the chain whose links a contradiction typology indexes; unresolved conflicts are its natural input.
    • LOF-ODP-005 places enactments in time for lex posterior and gives the periods during which a derogation is in force.
