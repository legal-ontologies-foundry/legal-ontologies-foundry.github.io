    LOF-ODP-003: Correlative Deontic Positions¶
    Identifier LOF-ODP-003
    IRI https://w3id.org/lof/odp-003.owl
    Repository https://github.com/legal-ontologies-foundry/lof-odp-003
    Status draft, with an open placement decision
    Version 0.1.0
    Depends on LOF-ODP-001
    Drafted by David R. Koepsell
    For review by William Mandrick, Barry Smith

    Decision required

    This pattern is published in two versions that differ only in the BFO placement of legal position. The founding coordinators are asked to choose between them. See the decision.

    Intent¶

    Wesley Hohfeld showed that the word "right" covers four different legal positions, each paired with a correlative held by another party:

    Position Correlative Order
    claim duty first-order: what parties must or may do
    liberty no-claim first-order
    power liability second-order: who can change first-order positions
    immunity disability second-order

    This pattern models the eight positions, their correlative pairing, their holders, the legal content that grounds them, and the legal acts in which powers are exercised. It gives every Foundry ontology a common vocabulary for rights and duties that is more precise than the word "right" alone.

    Competency questions¶

    1. What duties does this person hold, and to whom are they owed?
    2. What is the correlative of this claim, and who holds it?
    3. In virtue of what legal content does this position exist?
    4. Which legal acts were exercises of this power?
    5. Does this position survive the assignment of the claim, or the death of its holder?

    Solution¶

    Classes¶

    Label IRI Parent Correlative
    legal position ODP003_0001000 see decision
    first-order legal position ODP003_0001009 legal position
    second-order legal position ODP003_0001010 legal position
    claim (alt. right) ODP003_0001001 first-order duty
    duty ODP003_0001002 first-order claim
    liberty (alt. privilege) ODP003_0001003 first-order no-claim
    no-claim (alt. no-right) ODP003_0001004 first-order liberty
    power ODP003_0001005 second-order liability
    liability ODP003_0001006 second-order power
    immunity ODP003_0001007 second-order disability
    disability ODP003_0001008 second-order immunity

    Definitions for all eleven classes are in the ontology file. For example, duty: "A first-order legal position in virtue of which its holder is required to engage in, or refrain from, some conduct owed to another party."

    Relations¶

    Label IRI Definition
    held by ODP003_0001100 A relation between a legal position and the person or organization whose position it is.
    grounded in ODP003_0001101 A relation between a legal position and the legal content in virtue of which it exists.
    correlative of ODP003_0001102 A symmetric relation between two legal positions held by different parties, each of which exists if and only if the other does.
    exercised in ODP003_0001103 A relation between a power and a legal act through which the holder changes the legal positions of another party.

    Axioms (Manchester syntax, common to both options)¶

    legal position SubClassOf (held by some material entity)
                      and (grounded in some legal content)
                      and (correlative of some legal position)
claim SubClassOf  first-order legal position
            and (correlative of some duty) and (correlative of only duty)
duty  SubClassOf  first-order legal position
            and (correlative of some claim) and (correlative of only claim)
... likewise for the other three pairs ...
DisjointClasses: claim, duty, liberty, no-claim, power, liability, immunity, disability
correlative of: Symmetric

    The placement decision¶

    The two options differ in two axioms only.

    Option A (editors' file) Option B (alternatives/)
    legal position SubClassOf specifically dependent continuant (BFO:0000020) generically dependent continuant (BFO:0000031)
    held by functional subproperty of BFO inheres in ordinary relation, not functional
    A position is... a feature of its holder, like a role a piece of legal content's upshot, related to a holder
    Can a position change holders? No. It ends when its holder ceases to hold it. Yes. The same position can pass to a new holder.
    Assignment of a claim the old claim ends; a new claim begins in the assignee the same claim is now held by the assignee
    Death of a debtor the duty ends; the estate bears a new duty the same duty passes to the estate
    Fit with ODP-002 uniform: roles and positions both inhere in bearers positions and roles are of different kinds
    Fit with ODP-001 positions are distinct from the content grounding them positions are close in kind to legal content

    Held by is functional in Option A because BFO's axioms state that a specifically dependent continuant has exactly one bearer. BFO's OWL file does not encode this, so the pattern states it explicitly.

    A test that separates them¶

    The repository includes a worked example (a residential lease) and an assignment test, in which the landlord assigns its claim to rent to a collection agency, and the agency is said to hold the same claim. make test-options reasons over both:

    Lease example Same claim after assignment
    Option A consistent inconsistent
    Option B consistent consistent

    So the choice is not merely metaphysical. It determines how every Foundry ontology must represent assignment, succession, subrogation, and novation. Under Option A, these are all modeled as the ending of one position and the beginning of another, linked by a legal act. Under Option B, they are changes in the holder of a single persisting position.

    Considerations¶

    For Option A. It treats legal positions uniformly with legal roles (ODP-002). It keeps a clear line between the content of the law, which is generically dependent, and what the law brings about in particular persons, which is specifically dependent. It also matches ordinary legal talk of a person having a duty in the way they have a role. Novation, where a new obligation replaces the old, fits it naturally.

    For Option B. Legal practice often treats a claim as a transferable asset, the same claim passing from assignor to assignee. Option B represents that directly, without multiplying positions. It also aligns with approaches that treat obligations as directive information content, such as the Common Core Ontologies' directive information content entities.

    Declaration of interest. The drafter's own work (A Structural Ontology of the Law) treats obligations as specifically dependent continuants, as in Option A. Option A is placed in the editors' file only so that the pattern has a working default; it carries no presumption.

    Example¶

    ex:anaRentDuty a odp3:0001002 ;            # duty
    odp3:0001100 ex:ana ;                  # held by
    odp3:0001101 ex:leaseTerms ;           # grounded in
    odp3:0001102 ex:northsideRentClaim .   # correlative of

ex:northsideRentClaim a odp3:0001001 ;     # claim
    odp3:0001100 ex:northside ;
    odp3:0001101 ex:leaseTerms .

ex:northsideTerminationPower a odp3:0001005 ;   # power
    odp3:0001100 ex:northside ;
    odp3:0001101 ex:tenantCode ;
    odp3:0001102 ex:anaTerminationLiability .

    Alignment with legal standards (LOF-P-015)¶

    The eight positions follow Hohfeld's Fundamental Legal Conceptions (1913, 1917). LKIF-Core and LegalRuleML represent obligations and permissions as deontic modalities or rule components, rather than as entities with holders, so their mapping to this pattern is partial. It will be documented once the placement question is settled.

    Consequences and limitations¶

    • The pattern does not say what a position is about: the action type a duty requires (paying rent) or a liberty permits. Referring to types of action is a known difficulty in OWL; the options are listed below.
    • OWL cannot express that correlatives are held by different parties, or that a claim and its correlative duty concern the same action. These are documented constraints, not enforced ones.
    • Hohfeld's jural opposites (claim versus no-claim in the same holder) are not modeled, because they relate a position to the absence of another.

    Open questions for the founding coordinators¶

    1. Placement. Option A or Option B? (The central question.)
    2. Action types. How should a position indicate the conduct it concerns? Options: (a) a relation to a plan specification or directive information content entity, following IAO and CCO; (b) a relation to a process universal via OWL punning; (c) a relation to a process profile. Each has costs for reasoning.
    3. Realizability. Should duties, liberties, and powers be treated as realizable entities, realized in compliance, in acting, and in the exercise of the power? This is available only under Option A.
    4. Should correlatives be modeled as parts of a single relational whole (a legal relation between two parties), rather than as two linked positions?

    Related patterns¶

    • LOF-ODP-001 supplies legal content (grounding) and legal act (exercise of powers).
    • LOF-ODP-002: conferring a legal role is typically the exercise of a power, and a role is typically specified by the positions its holder acquires.
